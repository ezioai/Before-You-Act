# Check the report's sources

[Start here](README.md) · [Worked example](scenario.md) · [Craft a good prompt](responses.md) · **Check sources** · [Workflow template](decision-brief.md)

> **Your Ezio report already lists its sources under each claim.** Your job is to open a couple of the cited pages and confirm they actually back your decision—not to build a bibliography.

## After your analysis

- Read the findings, then open the relevant **Sources** links under each claim in the report.
- **Citations are leads, not proof.** A cited page may cover a different product, place, or case than yours. Confirm the page addresses **your** topic before relying on it; record mismatches as findings.
- Choose **two useful passages** for your workflow. They may support, contradict, or limit a claim; both can come from one authoritative source; you do not have to find an error.
- Record the passage or section, the link, and what it means for the decision. One supported claim does not clear a whole recommendation.
- If sources conflict, show what would resolve the conflict. If a cited page is inaccessible, record the unresolved check and your next action—do not claim to have read it.

| Your decision is about | An authoritative page usually comes from | Check the scope |
|---|---|---|
| A purchase | Manufacturer specifications; seller warranty terms | Exact model, region, condition, which seller's terms apply |
| A subscription or plan | Official feature documentation and plan terms | Plan tier, current version, limits, eligibility |
| A visit or booking | Official visitor information or fare terms | Date, opening hours, ticket conditions, refund rules |
| A payment or charge | The official rule or statute | Jurisdiction, effective dates, exceptions |

## Make each citation useful

Record a title/link, section or short passage, access date, and the connection to your decision. Useful pattern:

> "This passage establishes `____`. It does not establish `____`, so workflow step `__` asks `____`."

Check jurisdiction, dates, definitions, and whether the page actually concerns your case. Neither Ezio, a search snippet, nor a source's authority can verify private facts that were never supplied.

## Trouble opening a source?

Open it in a normal browser or ask an organizer for help. A paywall or missing page is not proof that a claim is false—use another cited source or an unresolved-check branch.
