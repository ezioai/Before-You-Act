# Before You Act

**An AI gives you a confident answer. Should you act on it?**

> **Define your objective + criteria → ask an AI → check it in Ezio → summarize → decide.**

## The five steps

### Step 0 — Define your problem and criteria

Pick one small, everyday decision—no personal details. A refurbished laptop to buy, a software plan to pick, a trip to plan, a deposit to question.

Then write your **decision criteria**: the facts that would actually change your answer.

- **Decision Criteria 1:** `____`
- **Decision Criteria 2:** `____`
- (optional) **Decision Criteria 3:** `____`

No personal medical, legal, immigration, investment, or emergency situations.

### Step I — Create prompts and collect answers

Ask ChatGPT or any AI tool you already use about your criteria. Save the **exact answer** and where it came from. [How to craft a good prompt](responses.md).

### Step II — Validate the response in Ezio

Paste only the AI answer into [Ezio](https://app.ezioai.io)—not your setup or instructions. One analysis is enough. Read the report against your criteria:

- **Supported:** evidence backs the claim. Not automatic permission to act.
- **Contradicted:** look at the conflicting evidence.
- **Not enough evidence:** unresolved—not true or false, just unverified.
- **Failed / not checked:** no completed assessment.

### Step III — Sequence of actions

For each finding, write the action you would take:

- **Act on contradictions** — what a Contradicted claim changes, and who does what next.
- **Act on "not enough evidence"** — treat unverified claims as open questions: name who could answer them and what happens while you wait.
- **Check a source or name the expert** — what action would you take with each? Your report lists its sources under each claim; citations are leads, not proof, so confirm a cited page is about **your** product, place, or rule and note **two useful passages** for your workflow. For anything only a person can settle, name who you'd ask and the question you'd send. [How to check them](sources.md).

Every action needs **a person, an action, and a deadline**. Use the [one-page template](decision-brief.md) — evidence, sequence of actions, trade-off, ask a human, next move.

### Step IV — Recommendation

Make your call based on the findings and your sequence of actions. Then run the **five-minute no-answer test**: suppose your most important question is still unanswered near the deadline. Follow your own steps—who does what next? Fix any dead end. You never actually contact anyone or wait; it's a thought experiment.

**Done? Click Feedback in Ezio's top menu** and fill in the form—tell us what helped, what surprised you, and what could be better. Positive, critical, and mixed feedback all count (and enter the raffle—see Quick reference below).

## See it done: the apartment example

Jordan's landlord wants a **$4,000 deposit on a $2,000/month apartment**, answer by tomorrow. An AI said a two-month deposit is "unlawful without checking anything else." Ezio supported the general rule but **contradicted the "no exceptions" claim**—California law has a small-landlord exception. Jordan's workflow isn't "refuse" or "pay"; it's *who determines which rule applies, and what to do if nobody answers by tomorrow*.

## Quick reference

<details>
<summary><strong>What do Ezio's findings mean?</strong></summary>

- **Supported:** evidence supports that claim—not automatic permission to act.
- **Contradicted:** inspect the conflicting evidence and its scope.
- **Not enough evidence:** unresolved, not automatically true or false.
- **Failed / unavailable / not checked:** no completed assessment for that statement.

Record actual findings. You may disagree with source-backed reasons. Keep the report unchanged and explain any disagreement in your workflow. A full Accuracy bar covers the claims that were scored—not every claim or the safety of the proposed action.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Best Use of Ezio vs. the feedback raffle</strong></summary>

**Best Use of Ezio:** one month free unlimited access; judged across all tracks, excluding overall 1st/2nd/3rd-place teams. Your existing submission is the evidence. Judges look for meaningful use, accurate interpretation, and a demonstrated improvement or justified confirmation. No bonus for praise or extra runs. Ties favor stronger evidence interpretation, then a recorded panel decision.

Optional note: **"What did Ezio help you change, verify, or decide—and where can judges see it?"** Write 2–3 sentences in the PDF appendix.

**Feedback raffle:** optional and random, open to eligible attendees across tracks, including podium winners. A live challenge run counts; no extra exercise. Click **Feedback** in Ezio's top menu to open the form. Positive, critical, and mixed feedback have equal odds and no effect on judging. Follow the announced cutoff, prize, and terms; do not delay your challenge submission.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Fair play and help if something fails</strong></summary>

Build your workflow during 1:15–3:00 PM. Record the answer's origin honestly. Existing non-sensitive AI answers are allowed as source material, not pre-built workflows. Ask before correcting an input; disclose approved changes and use the matching final report. Never fabricate evidence or claim rewritten text is a verbatim AI answer.

Use your approved account; never share passwords. Disclose other tools/assistance and do your own reasoning. No real leases, passport details, financial records, personal or confidential material. Draft only: do not contact anyone, pay, book, sign, or publish.

For access/export failures, ask an organizer rather than repeatedly retrying.

</details>
